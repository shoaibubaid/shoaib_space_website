
Ya‘qub ibn ‘Utba (يعقوب بن عتبة بن المغيرة بن الأخنس الثقفي, Ya‘qub b. ‘Utba b. al-Mughira b. al-Akhnas al-Thaqafi) was an 8th-century Medinan scholar, jurist (faqīh), and one of the primary historical informants used by Ibn Ishaq in compiling the Sīrah (Prophetic biography).

1. Identity and Lineage
Tribe: Banu Thaqif (confederated with Quraysh / Banu Zuhrah in Medina).

Lineage: His great-grandfather was al-Akhnas b. Shariq al-Thaqafi, an influential Qurayshi ally and chieftain in pre-Islamic Mecca.

Era & Death: A member of the Tabi‘un (Successor generation), he lived in Medina and died in 128 AH (c. 745–746 CE).

2. Role in Early Islamic Historiography
Primary Source for Ibn Ishaq: Ibn Ishaq frequently opens historical and genealogical reports with "Ya‘qub b. ‘Utba b. al-Mughira b. al-Akhnas told me..." (حدثني يعقوب بن عتبة).

Preservation of Pre-Islamic & Early Prophetic Lore: Ya‘qub was particularly renowned for his deep mastery of Sīrah and Maghāzī (historical biographies, campaign chronicles, and pre-Islamic Arabian tribal events). Much of our textual transmission regarding the late Himyarite kingdom, the Year of the Elephant, and early Meccan genealogy passed through his chains of narration.

3. Reputation and Scholarship
Scholarly Reliability: Classical biographical authorities (such as Ibn Sa‘d, Yahya b. Ma‘in, and al-Dhahabi) classified him as a trustworthy narrator (thiqah), notable for his legal knowledge (fiqh) and piety.

Public Service: In Medina, the local administration frequently entrusted him with supervising and distributing charitable revenues (sadaqāt) due to his reputation for integrity.
