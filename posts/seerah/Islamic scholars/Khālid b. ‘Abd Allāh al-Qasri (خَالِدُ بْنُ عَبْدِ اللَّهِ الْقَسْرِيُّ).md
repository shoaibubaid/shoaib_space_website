**Later Scholar / Political Figure**

**Timeline:** Died 126 AH (743 CE).

**Brief intro:** He was a prominent and controversial governor of Iraq (serving both Kufa and Basra) during the Umayyad Caliphate under Caliph Hisham b. 'Abd al-Malik. While he was a political leader rather than a traditional religious scholar, he is a major figure in the historical reports of that era.

In the context of the scholars you are researching (**Wahb b. Munabbih**, **al-Suddi**, and **Qatada**), Khalid is significant for the following:

- **Patron of Scholars:** He was a contemporary of the major Tabi'in and often interacted with them. He is famously known for his interest in the history of the ancient Arabs and the Israelites, often commissioning scholars like **Wahb b. Munabbih** to record historical accounts.
    
- **The Sacrifice of Ja'd b. Dirham:** He is most infamous in religious history for executing Ja'd b. Dirham (the teacher of Jahm b. Safwan) on the day of Eid al-Adha, claiming he was "sacrificing" a heretic who denied that Allah spoke to Moses or took Abraham as a friend.
    
- **Administrative Legacy:** He was known for his massive engineering projects, including digging canals in Iraq to improve agriculture, reflecting the same interest in water management seen in the histories of the **Ma’rib Dam**.
