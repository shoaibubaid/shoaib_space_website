## A kingdom built on water

Long before Makkah became the center of the story, southern Arabia was home to the kingdom of Saba — known today as Sheba. Its wealth came from something unusual for a desert region: engineering. The kingdom controlled the incense trade routes and, more importantly, mastered irrigation in a land that received almost no rain.

## The Ma'rib dam

The centerpiece of that engineering was the Ma'rib dam, an enormous earthen structure that captured seasonal floodwater and distributed it across surrounding farmland. For centuries it sustained orchards, vineyards, and a population large enough to be described in later tradition as living among "gardens on the right and the left."

![Illustration of an ancient irrigated valley](../../assets/floats/monitor_cute.png)

## The flood that ended an era

Over generations the dam weakened, whether from neglect, an earthquake, or simple wear is still debated. When it finally broke, the flood — remembered as *Sayl al-Arim* — destroyed the farmland the kingdom depended on. Tribes scattered north across the peninsula, some eventually settling in and around Makkah and Madinah, carrying the memory of Saba's rise and fall with them.
