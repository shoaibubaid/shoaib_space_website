[[Tabi]]. Maʿrūf bin Suwayd was a **Tabiʿī** (the generation that followed the Sahaba). He is primarily known as a narrator of **Hadith**, particularly from the prominent companion **Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī** (أَبُو ذَرٍّ الْغِفَارِيُّ).

One of his most famous narrations recorded in **Sahih Muslim** describes Abū Dharr wearing a garment identical to the one his servant was wearing. When Maʿrūf asked about it, Abū Dharr explained that the Prophet ﷺ had commanded that servants should be treated like brothers—fed what the masters eat and clothed with what they wear.

Because he lived during the transition from the era of the Sahaba to the era of documentation, his narrations provide a bridge for historians looking at the behavior and ethics of the early **Ghifār**.
