 **Later Scholar.**

His full name is **Abū al-Khaṭṭāb ʿUmar ibn al-Ḥasan** (أَبُو الْخَطَّابِ عُمَرُ بْنُ الْحَسَنِ). He was a highly renowned 12th- to 13th-century scholar, historian, and expert in Hadith from **Al-Andalus** (Islamic Spain). 

- **Claim to Fame**: He claimed to be a direct descendant of the handsome Sahabi **Diḥyah al-Kalbī** (دِحْيَةُ الْكَلْبِيُّ) — the famous companion in whose human form the Angel Jibril often appeared. Because of this, he is also frequently referred to in literature as **Ibn Diḥyah al-Kalbī**.
- **His Travels**: He was a globetrotter who moved from Spain to North Africa, then traversed through Egypt, Syria, and Iraq to collect knowledge.
- **The First Dar al-Hadith**: In Egypt, the Ayyubid Sultan Al-Kamil respected his vast memory so much that he appointed him as the first director of Cairo's famous specialized Hadith school, the _Kāmilīyah_.
- **Literary Legacy**: He wrote heavily on the biography and special characteristics of the Prophet. If you see him in literature, it is often as an authoritative reference for early Arab poetry or detailed Prophetic traits.
