Al-Zuhrī (ابن شهاب الزهري, Ibn Shihāb al-Zuhrī, c. 58–124 AH / 678–742 CE) was one of the foremost foundational scholars in Islamic history, universally recognized as the central pioneer in the formal codification of Hadith and Sīrah (Prophetic biography).

### 1. Identity and Lineage
- Full Name: Muḥammad ibn Muslim ibn ‘Ubaydullāh ibn ‘Abdullāh ibn Shihāb al-Zuhrī (محمد بن مسلم بن عبيد الله بن عبد الله بن شهاب الزهري).

- Tribal Clan: He belonged to Banū Zuhrah, a prestigious, noble clan of Quraysh in Mecca (the maternal clan of the Prophet’s mother, Āminah bint Wahb, and the clan of the companion Sa‘d ibn Abī Waqqāṣ).

- Generation: He was a prominent member of the younger Tābi‘ūn (Successors), having studied directly under several senior Companions of the Prophet (such as Anas ibn Mālik, Sahl ibn Sa‘d, and ‘Abdullāh ibn ‘Umar), as well as the famous Seven Jurists of Medina (notably ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr and Sa‘īd ibn al-Musayyib).

### 2. Father of the Written Sīrah
- The Chronological Framework: Before al-Zuhrī, reports of the Prophet’s campaigns (Maghāzī) and pre-Islamic tribal lore circulated largely as disjointed oral traditions. Al-Zuhrī was among the very first scholars to compile these scattered accounts into a structured, chronological historical narrative.

- Teacher of Ibn Isḥāq: He was the primary mentor and source for Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq (the author of the foundational Sīrat Rasūl Allāh upon which Ibn Hishām and Ibn Kathīr rely). Whenever you read Ibn Kathīr quoting a continuous chain running through Ibn Isḥāq from al-Zuhrī, you are reading the historical backbone of the early Islamic biographical tradition.

### 3. The Official Codification of Hadith
- The Imperial Mandate: Al-Zuhrī is historically credited as the first person to formally record Hadith in book form on an official state level.

- Umayyad Patronage: The Umayyad caliph ‘Umar ibn ‘Abd al-‘Azīz (‘Umar II, r. 99–101 AH) feared the loss of Prophetic traditions as the older generation of scholars passed away. He issued an official decree commanding al-Zuhrī to travel, gather, and write down the authentic traditions of the Prophet into compendiums, which were then distributed to the provincial capitals.

### 4. Intellectual Profile & Reputation
- Photographic Memory: Classical biographers (such as al-Dhahabī and Ibn Khallikān) famously noted his extraordinary memory; it was said he never forgot a single report or genealogical chain once he heard it.

- Rigorous Use of the Isnād: Al-Zuhrī was instrumental in institutionalizing the isnād (chain of transmitters), famously insisting that a historical or legal statement was worthless unless grounded in a verified chain of reputable authorities.

- Scholarly Standing: Imām Mālik ibn Anas (who was al-Zuhrī’s direct student) said of him: "This knowledge is a religion, so look to whom you take your religion from. There remained in Medina no one with greater knowledge of the Sunnah than al-Zuhrī."
