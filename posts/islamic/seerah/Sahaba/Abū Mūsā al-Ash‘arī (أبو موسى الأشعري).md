# Abū Mūsā al-Ash‘arī (أبو موسى الأشعري, ‘Abd Allāh b. Qays, d. c. 42–52 AH / 662–672 CE)

- Lineage & Tribe: Banū al-Ash‘ar, a major South Arabian (Qaḥṭānite / Yemeni) tribe.

- Arrival: Accepted Islam in Mecca before migrating to Abyssinia, later arriving in Medina with his tribe aboard a ship (Aṣḥāb al-Safīnah) around the Battle of Khaybar (7 AH).

- Key Role: Renowned for his beautiful Qur'anic recitation (described as possessing "one of the flutes of the family of David") and administrative competence.

- Legacy: Sent by the Prophet alongside Mu‘ādh to govern and teach the people of Yemen. Later served as governor of Basra and Kūfah under ‘Umar and ‘Uthmān, played a key military role in the conquest of Persia (Khuzestan), and served as the representative for ‘Alī's camp during the Arbitration of the Battle of Ṣiffīn.
