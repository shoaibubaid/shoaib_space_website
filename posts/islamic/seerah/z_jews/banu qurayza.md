**Banu Qurayza** was one of the three major Jewish tribes living in Medina (then known as Yathrib) before and during the rise of Islam. They played a significant role in the changing political landscape of the city.

### **Who Were They?**

- **The Big Three:** Alongside the **Banu Qaynuqa** and **Banu al-Nadir**, the Banu Qurayza were principal inhabitants of Medina’s rich agricultural oasis.
    
- **Lifestyle:** Unlike nomadic Bedouins, they lived in heavily fortified stone fortresses (_āṭām_) situated in the southeastern outskirts of the oasis, where they controlled vast date palm plantations and excelled in metallurgy, agriculture, and weapons trading.

### **Their Position in Medina's Conflicts**

To understand why they are mentioned in the stories about the Yemeni King Tubba‘ and the Ansar, you have to look at how tribal politics worked back then:

1. **The Original Power Dynamic:** Historically, the Jewish tribes arrived in Medina first and held the upper hand economically and militarily. When the Arab tribes of **al-Aws** and **al-Khazraj** (the future Ansar) migrated north from Yemen, they initially lived as client-allies under the Jewish tribes.
    
2. **The Shift in Power:** Over time, with help from regional allies (like the stories of King Tubba‘ or other regional chieftains intervening), the Arab Aws and Khazraj managed to flip the script and take dominant political control of Medina.
    
3. **The Alliances:** To survive the brutal civil wars that constantly broke out in Medina (such as the famous Battle of Bu'ath), the Jewish tribes split their allegiances. The Banu Qurayza and Banu al-Nadir allied themselves with the **al-Aws** tribe, while the Banu Qaynuqa allied with **al-Khazraj**.
